\chapter{Music Consumption Survey}\label{ch:survey}

To better support the choice of topic and demonstrate that a music streaming platform
centered on social interactions could be a relevant service, a brief survey was conducted.
It examined individual music listening and discovery habits, platform usage patterns,
music-related interactions between people, and attitudes
towards potential social features on the platform.


\section{Methods}
The service chosen for the questionnaire was Google Forms~\parencite{googleforms}.
It provides a simple interface for survey creation, allows easy sharing of the form,
and supports extracting results as CSV files.

The questionnaire consists of 15 questions.
Most of them are multiple-choice and closed-ended, with some allowing a custom answer.
All custom answers are grouped under the ``other'' category in the provided figures.
Answers in their original form can be found in the full survey.

The complete questionnaire is included in the thesis repository under
\path{text/extras/survey-questions.pdf} and online.\footnote{\url{https://docs.google.com/forms/d/1vhhAu_SfuHV4xy6JoDaRsM5uCElYn_csTmN8u4ZlpHc}}


\section{Results}
This section presents the survey outcomes and analyzes their significance.
Not all questions are discussed. For example, some questions about music discovery are omitted.

A total of 119 people participated, with the majority being in the 18 to 30 age group,
with the exact distribution shown in \path{text/charts/age.png}.

\subsection{Engagement}
Firstly, it was necessary to determine whether respondents listen
to music frequently and whether they use streaming platforms for this purpose.

Over 50\% of respondents reported listening to more than 500 songs per month, i.e.,
16 songs a day on average (\path{text/charts/song amount month.png}),
and nearly 80\% stated that they listen to music daily (\path{text/charts/listening regularity.png}).

The numbers show that people have a strong interest in music, and
the next step was to determine how the audio content is mostly accessed.

\subsection{Listening Methods}
As shown in \path{text/charts/constumption_method.png}, the proportion of respondents using streaming
platforms across all age groups is approaching 100\%, with slightly higher
numbers in the younger age groups.
Although downloaded files and physical media are still used,
especially among people aged 30 to 60, they are usually only a secondary option next to streaming solutions.

The question about the popularity of individual platforms showed that Spotify is the most used one,
which aligns with global statistics~\parencite{spotifypopularity}.
However, Yandex Music, coming in second place, deserves further explanation.
Most of the respondents were from Russian-speaking countries, specifically Russia, and,
with many Western companies leaving the Russian market in 2022, including music streaming services~\parencite{spotify_russia_exit},
most users have moved to locally available products: Yandex Music~\parencite{yandex_music}, VK Music~\parencite{vk_music}, and
Zvuk~\parencite{zvuk}.
This also explains why other popular region-specific platforms,
such as Amazon Music~(US), QQ Music~(China),
and JioSaavn~(India), are absent from the survey.

The complete statistics are shown in \path{text/charts/streaming platform.png}.

The survey showed that streaming services are indeed widely used.
The next important step was to analyze the regularity of music-centered social interactions.

\subsection{Social Interactions}
The results of Question 10 indicate that nearly all respondents (99.2\%) reported listening to music with
others at least sometimes, primarily in offline settings such as gatherings
or car journeys (\path{text/charts/listen together method.png}).
Online collaborative listening methods, although less common, were also mentioned by a quarter
of respondents.

Question 11 (\path{text/charts/listen together freq.png}) examines the frequency of these interactions:
the results show that a significant portion of respondents engage in shared listening regularly.
Around 60\% reported doing so at least a few times per month,
with a smaller group indicating almost daily interactions.
This supports the previously mentioned idea in \chapterref{ch:intro}
that music consumption is a common social activity.

The next question asks: ``How often do you/your friends share/discuss music?''.
While the previous question focused on real-time in-person collective listening,
this one looks at more intentional and active musical interactions, such as sharing songs,
recommending music, or discussing it.
Here, music is the primary purpose of engagement, rather than background.
The same trend as before can be observed in the responses:
over 70\% (\path{text/charts/share freq.png}) indicated that they share music at least several
times a month, with many doing so weekly or more often.
This further confirms the active social role of music.

Question 13, ``How does that happen?'' (in terms of music sharing),
asks about the specific ways people share music.
Many respondents (\path{text/charts/share method.png}) stated that they typically send links
from streaming platforms through external messaging apps.
While this shows an apparent demand for sharing music online, it also reveals a gap among platforms,
as these interactions remain fragmented.
Hence, enabling such exchanges directly within the streaming app could provide a smoother
and more uniform experience.

Responses to the question ``Would you say that you know a lot of people with similar music taste?''
were mixed, with a near-even split.
This suggests that while some users already have a social circle with similar music taste, many do not.
In addition, when asked
``Would you like to connect with others who share your musical taste, or influence those around you to explore and appreciate the music you enjoy?'',
the majority answered positively.

This data further supports the idea that social discovery features could be useful within a streaming platform.
Moreover, over 60\% of participants stated in Question 15 that they would use
additional social features if available on a streaming platform.


\section{Summary}
Overall, the results indicate that people listen to music frequently,
with streaming services being their primary medium of accessing it.
The data also suggest that music plays an important role in everyday life
and often appears in social situations.

Yet, existing platforms only partly support interaction between users.
This makes the idea of a music streaming service with built-in social features both relevant and
likely to address real user needs.
