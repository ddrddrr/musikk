\chapter{Existing Platforms}\label{ch:platforms}

In this chapter, existing platforms and services are examined to gain a better understanding
of the tools people use when interacting with music.
Additionally, this chapter provides insight
into the potential features that could be implemented in the resulting application.

Firstly, streaming services are discussed, as the main aim of the thesis is to create
a system for music streaming.
Then, other music-related services~(e.g., forums) are presented,
as they offer alternative ways of engaging with audio content.
Some of those ideas could be useful for the future platform.
Lastly, platforms such as YouTube and TikTok are examined,
because the survey has shown that they are a popular way to interact with music~(\path{text/charts/discovery methods.png}).


\section{Streaming Services}
As already illustrated in \path{text/charts/constumption_method.png},
and further confirmed by the study of the International Federation of
the Phonographic Industry~\parencite{music_stats_2024},
the most common way of music consumption and discovery is through streaming services.
Although many options are available, this section only considers those that are both popular and
have unique features.
The descriptions, rather than offering a general description of each platform,
will focus on the service-specific social features.

\subsection{Spotify}
One of the most prominent social features on Spotify is the ``Spotify Jam'' feature~\parencite{spotify_jam}.
It lets people create a collective song queue that is then synchronized among all connected users.
Moreover, volume, the order of songs, and other playback aspects can be controlled individually by each user.

Another notable feature is ``Friend Activity''~\parencite{spotify_friend_activ},
which shows what the followers of a given user are currently listening to.

Another feature is ``Listening Parties'': these are live chats with a limited capacity
that can be joined for a short time when new music is being released~\parencite{spotify_party_1,spotify_party_2}.
This brings the offline collective music discovery experience online.

During the implementation of this thesis~(the text was initially written in spring to summer 2025),
Spotify has also added support for user-to-user chats~(from September 2025)~\parencite{spotify_chat}.
This further supports the claim that social features are in demand on music platforms.

\subsection{VK Music}
VK Music is a music service integrated into the VK social network.
Consequently, it is possible to send songs and playlists via private messages or add them to
posts in groups.
VK Music also provides an ``Updates'' tab, which is populated by the audio materials that
any followed user or group has added to their ``Liked Feed''~\parencite{vk_music}.

\subsection{SoundCloud}
SoundCloud is one of the few platforms that lets its users leave comments
and reactions on songs and playlists~\parencite{sc_comments,sc_reactions}.
In addition, each user has a feed consisting of personal uploads and
reposts of others' content~\parencite{sc_reposts}, which is visible when visiting their profile.

\subsection{Bandcamp}
Every Bandcamp user has a profile with four tabs: ``collection'',
``wishlist'', ``followers'', and ``following''.~\parencite{bandcamp_fans}
A useful feature is under the ``following'' tab: users can subscribe to available genres and then
specify the ones they want to show to others viewing their profile.
The ``wishlist'' tab is also useful,
as it lets the users show what they are looking forward to listening to in the future.

This concludes the streaming services section.
Services that are focused on music, but not on the streaming itself, are discussed next.


\section{Forums, Blogs, and Other Music Media}
Different resources are gathered under these umbrella terms, from professional music review websites
to amateur and personal pages.
Again, only widely used services that offer something noteworthy are listed.

\subsection{Rate Your Music}

\begin{quote}
    ``Rate Your Music is one of the largest music databases online. It is an incredible tool that
    can help you find and learn about new music to listen to.''~\parencite{ryt}
\end{quote}
This website is one of the largest platforms with user-created content.
Every member can write reviews and set ratings for music releases,
contribute to the ``wiki'' consisting of genres, thematic lists, and charts,
and connect with other members of the forum.

\subsection{last.fm}
Last.fm tracks listening statistics and helps with music discovery.
However, it also has a couple of notable social features~\parencite{lastfm_features}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Global and personal events pages.
    In the former, users are able to browse the upcoming events near their location, and in the latter
    they are able to mark which events they plan to attend.
    Other users can then see, either on the event page or on that user's profile,
    which events the user is interested in and/or plans to attend.
    \item A ``Neighbours'' tab.
    The ``Neighbours'' tab shows other users with similar listening habits,
    based on shared artists and an overall compatibility score.
    \item ``Shouts'' section.
    The ``Shouts'' section is a profile-scoped feed, where the users can leave comments
    and reply to what others have written.
\end{enumerate}

This section has introduced a couple of possible features not previously seen
in the listed streaming services, such as discussion forums and profile feeds.

The following section examines platforms that are not primarily focused
on music but still influence how people engage with it.


\section{General-Purpose Platforms}
As mentioned earlier, one of the most interesting results of the survey is the high number of people
discovering music on platforms that are not focused on music.
Services like Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok are primarily used for sharing videos
and other user-generated content. They have become closely tied to how people find
music and listen to it.
In recent years, these platforms have started to promote music more directly by allowing users to
embed tracks within their own content.

Instagram, for example, added a special audio tool that lets users include music in their short
videos (``Reels''~\parencite{inst_audio}), helping songs reach more people through visual content.

YouTube automatically identifies songs used in videos and displays them in the description,
making it easier to find and listen to them.~\parencite{youtube_music_detection}

TikTok has had an even bigger impact: many songs have gone viral and later became hits
in charts and streaming apps because people use
them in popular videos~(these are often called ``TikTok Hits'').~\parencite{tiktok_billboard}

This shows that big platforms are not just reacting to users' interest in music, they
actively help promote it by connecting songs with content that people want to watch and share.

An integration with such services would benefit the platform.
A possible feature could be a badge stating~``This track was in a TikTok that user Alice123 sent you today!''.

Overall, many music-related platforms with social
features built in are available across the Internet.
However, services that focus on the actual audio delivery often support just a subset of the
possible features.
This, in turn, forces users to use multiple platforms to meet their needs.
This project aims to bridge that gap, gathering multiple social features in one app.

Based on the research done in the previous and current chapters, the following social features were chosen
for implementation, as they provide a good balance between the complexity of implementation
and the level of social interaction:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Chat.
    \item Personal/Global Feed.\footnote{In comparison to \texttt{last.fm}, a common view with posts
    from multiple authors is also provided.}
    \item Song/Playlist Comments.
    \item Friends' Listening Activity.
\end{itemize}

These and further requirements for the application are listed in \chapterref{ch:specification}.
