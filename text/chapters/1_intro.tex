\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch:intro}

Music streaming has been steadily growing in popularity over the past
decade.~\parencite{music_stats_decade} This growth is not surprising, as music
plays an important role in people's lives.
For example, a recent psychological study suggests that music can influence mood,
change how people see themselves, and help them connect with others.~\parencite{music_role_life}
Its social importance is further demonstrated by the popularity of clubs, music
festivals, concerts, and other music-related events, where people often listen
to music together.~\parencite{concert_going_social_aspects}

Music platforms also reflect this social role of music, as many of them already include social features.
However, these features are usually limited, for instance, one platform may let users follow
each other, another may offer collaborative playlists, while a third may only
support comments on songs.
As shown in \chapterref{ch:platforms}, no reviewed platform incorporates a wide
set of social features within a single service.
The survey performed for this thesis~(\chapterref{ch:survey}) also supports this
idea, as most respondents said they would use additional social features on a
music platform.

To address this gap, this bachelor's thesis aims to develop a web-based
music management and streaming platform focused on social interactions
between users. The resulting platform, available at \url{https://musikk.stream},
combines music management, audio streaming, and social interaction in one
service.\footnote{The source code is also available at \url{https://github.com/ddrddrr/musikk/}.}

This work is divided into the following chapters:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \chapterref{ch:survey}
    Presents the outcomes of a survey illustrating how people consume music,
    how prevalent it is in social interactions, and why this type of platform can be relevant.

    \item \chapterref{ch:platforms}
    Explores which social features existing streaming platforms and other music-related services already offer.

    \item \chapterref{ch:specification}
    Defines the functional and non-functional requirements for the application.

    \item \chapterref{ch:planning}
    Describes the choices of technologies and architectural patterns used by the application.

    \item \chapterref{ch:implementation}
    Describes the development process and implementation details.

    \item \chapterref{ch:user_testing_improvements}
    Reports on user testing of the deployed application and lists known gaps and further improvements.

    \item \chapterref{ch:conclusion}
    Summarizes the outcome of the work.

    \item \hyperref[ch:appendix]{Appendix}
    Collects extended technical material referenced from the
    implementation chapter, in particular the audio Transcoding pipeline
    details.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Conventions used in this thesis:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Figures} \\
    Most of the diagrams and survey charts in this thesis are too large to embed
    inline. They are referenced by their file path in the project
    repository, for example \path{text/charts/age.png}
    or \path{text/diagrams/HighLevelSystem.png}.
    The complete sets are kept under \path{text/diagrams/}
    and \path{text/charts/}.

    \item \textbf{Citations} \\
    Sources are cited on the first mention of a work, term, or claim.
    Later mentions of the same work or term are not re-cited.
\end{itemize}
